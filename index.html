    FitWay | COREO · Case study

    Making coach pay make sense.

    A transparent, role-based performance and incentive system for COREO Gym, connected across FitWay CRM and the COREO Coach App.

    Note: Screens use sample names, numbers and calculations.

    TL;DR

    • Problem COREO Gym had no fair, consistent way to measure coaches or explain their incentives.
    • What I did Designed a connected experience: configuration and dashboards in FitWay CRM, and a personal performance view plus an incentive calculator in the COREO Coach App.
    • Outcome Live in FitWay CRM and the COREO Coach App: one transparent 100-point system that management can tune and coaches can understand.

    Project Snapshot

    CompanyFitWay
    ProductCOREO Gym
    My RoleUI/UX Designer
    PlatformsWeb CRM/Admin & Mobile App
    Primary UsersGym Management, Sales Team & Coaches
    ScopeCoach Performance Management
    StatusLive

    The Business Challenge

    COREO Gym wanted a fair, structured way to reward coaches. Management struggled with floor availability, PT revenue and inconsistent evaluation, and coaches had no clear view of how their effort turned into pay.

    COREO Gym wanted to move beyond conventional coach incentive practices and introduce a structured way to measure performance and reward contributions.

    Although coaches expected performance-based incentives, management faced challenges with accountability, gym-floor availability, PT revenue generation, and consistent performance evaluation.

    Some coaches completed their personal training sessions and left the gym floor before fulfilling their expected responsibilities. Others authenticated their attendance but spent time away from the floor.

    Management also lacked a structured way to evaluate revenue generation, session utilisation, and team contribution.

    With future franchise expansion in mind, COREO needed a more transparent and consistent performance management approach.

    The Goal
    Create a system that connects coach performance with measurable evaluation criteria and transparent incentives, while giving management and coaches the right level of information and control.

    My Role & Approach

    I worked closely with the gym owner, stakeholders, developers, and testing team throughout the design process.

    My contributions included:

    • Participating in stakeholder discussions to understand business requirements, operational constraints, and delivery timelines.
    • Reviewing the existing FitWay CRM and identifying where the Coach Performance module could fit within its dashboard section.
    • Conducting secondary research to understand the relationships between different performance parameters and business data.
    • Creating initial paper wireframes to explore the experience across web and mobile interfaces.
    • Refining the configuration interface and designing performance dashboards and coach-facing screens.
    • Exploring design iterations to make complex performance information easier to understand.
    • Collaborating with developers to assess design feasibility and with the testing team to validate expected behaviour.

    The initial configuration structure was established by the development team. My contribution focused on refining the interface, improving information presentation, and making the experience more understandable for its users.

    How the System Works

    Management sets the rules in FitWay CRM, the system scores every coach out of 100 across five parameters, and coaches see their own score and incentive in the COREO Coach App.

    The experience follows three simple stages:

    Configure → Evaluate → View Performance

    Configure — FitWay CRM

    COREO Super Admin configures revenue slabs, performance weightage, scoring thresholds, and incentive rules.

    Evaluate — Coach Performance

    Coach performance is evaluated across five parameters, contributing to a total score of 100 points. The system uses the configured criteria to calculate performance-based incentives.

    View Performance — CRM & COREO Coach App

    Management and the Sales Team can access coach performance through their respective CRM logins, while coaches view their individual performance and potential incentives through the COREO Coach App.

    Understanding the Performance Framework

    Five parameters make up the 100-point score: PT Revenue (40), Attendance & Availability (20), PT Session Utilisation (20), Member Feedback (10) and Team Targets (10).

    The system evaluates coaches across five parameters, contributing to a total performance score of 100 points.

    Performance ParameterWeightage
    PT Revenue40 points
    Attendance & Availability20 points
    PT Session Utilisation20 points
    Member Feedback10 points
    Team Targets10 points
    Total100 points

    Each radar axis is scaled to its own maximum, so a 10-point parameter isn’t dwarfed by the 40-point one.

    Design decision — A fair radar chart

    Big and small parameters, read on the same scale.

    The challenge: the five parameters have very different maximums. PT Revenue is worth 40 points, Member Feedback only 10. On one shared scale, the small parameters would barely leave the centre, and a coach with perfect feedback would still look weak.

    My approach: I scaled each axis to its own maximum. The edge of every axis is that parameter’s full score, so the halfway mark means half of it: 20 of 40 for PT Revenue, 10 of 20 for Attendance, 5 of 10 for Member Feedback. The shape of the chart shows how balanced a coach is, and the exact points sit in the score breakdown next to it.

    I checked the approach with the developer. The chart supports a different maximum for each of the five parameters, so it keeps working if the weightage changes.

    Understanding the Data Inputs

    PT Revenue

    PT package purchase records in FitWay CRM contribute to the coach’s revenue performance.

    Attendance & Availability

    Attendance is captured through ESSL face-recognition devices connected to the gym’s flap barrier system. Coaches complete an entry and exit authentication cycle. The CRM uses paired IN/OUT punch records to calculate recorded time inside the gym, which contributes to attendance scoring.

    PT Session Utilisation

    Scored on session utilisation criteria defined by the product manager and stakeholders.

    Member Feedback

    Gym members provide feedback through the COREO Client App, contributing to the coach’s feedback score.

    Team Targets

    This parameter represents team contribution and collaboration, including supporting colleagues and assisting members on the gym floor. Its scoring rules were set by the product team.

    Admin Experience — FitWay CRM

    Turning business rules into configurable settings.

    I refined the configuration screens and designed the dashboard, so the COREO Super Admin can edit every threshold and see each coach’s score, slab, bonus and estimated incentive in one place. Sales gets view-only access.

    The FitWay CRM provides COREO Super Admin with a dedicated Coach Performance configuration experience.

    Management can configure:

    • General performance settings, including session capacity, maximum share, and default feedback score.
    • Base revenue tiers and corresponding share percentages.
    • Revenue score thresholds and points.
    • Attendance and availability score thresholds.
    • PT session utilisation criteria.
    • Member feedback scoring.
    • Team target scoring.
    • Performance bonus thresholds.

    The configuration interface uses editable minimum thresholds and corresponding points or percentages, allowing management to maintain the performance framework.

    Role-based access

    • COREO Super Admin: Can view, create, edit, update, and delete performance configurations.
    • Sales Team: Has view-only access to coach performance through the Sales login.
    • FitWay Super Admin: Does not have access to modify COREO’s performance configuration.

    The configuration is scoped to the COREO client/location rather than automatically applying to every FitWay customer.

    Coach Experience — COREO Coach App

    Giving coaches visibility into their own performance.

    Coaches see only their own data: total incentive, performance radar, base share slab and bonus criteria, in a few simple screens.

    The COREO Coach App provides coaches with an individual view of their performance, revenue, and incentive information.

    The experience includes:

    Performance Overview

    A radar chart visualises the five performance parameters and provides an overall performance score.

    Member Overview

    Coaches can view total members, active and inactive members, expired services, and personal training members.

    Upcoming Sessions

    A dedicated section displays upcoming personal training sessions and relevant client information.

    Base Share & Incentive Breakdown

    Coaches can understand their PT revenue slab, earned base share, performance bonus, and projected incentive information.

    Performance History

    Historical incentive information helps coaches review their previous earning patterns.

    Role-based Access

    Coaches can view their own performance information but cannot modify actual performance records, evaluation criteria, or incentive configurations.

    My Design Initiative — Incentive Calculator

    From simply showing earnings to exploring earning potential.

    Some coaches were worried about the new system. Showing past earnings wasn’t enough, so I proposed an incentive calculator where they can try “what if” PT collections and bonus levels and see their projected payout.

    During discussions with coaches, I noticed that some were concerned about the introduction of the new performance evaluation system.

    I realised that simply showing performance scores and earned incentives might not be enough. Coaches also needed transparency into how their future earnings could change with their performance.

    I introduced an incentive calculator that allows coaches to experiment with projected PT collections and incentive assumptions to understand their potential earnings.

    What coaches can do

    • Enter their projected PT collection.
    • Explore the applicable base-share percentage.
    • Enter a projected performance bonus percentage.
    • Calculate their potential incentive.
    • Understand how different earning scenarios affect their projected payout.

    The calculator operates independently of actual performance records and management-defined criteria.

    My intention was to make the new performance system feel more transparent and motivating rather than simply introducing another evaluation mechanism.

    Collaboration & Validation

    Throughout the design process, I collaborated with developers to understand technical constraints and ensure the proposed interfaces were feasible within the existing system.

    I also worked with the testing team to review expected behaviour against stakeholder requirements before approval.

    The designs were reviewed with stakeholders, approved, and are now live in FitWay CRM and the COREO Coach App.

    Key Takeaways

    • Transparency has to work for both sides: consistent evaluation for management, clear cause and effect for coaches.
    • Complex scoring becomes approachable when every number is shown with its slab, weight and next step.
    • Looking past the brief led to the incentive calculator, the feature coaches were most curious about.

    This project helped me understand how different business requirements can be translated into a structured product experience.

    My biggest learning was that performance transparency needs to work for both sides: management needs a consistent way to evaluate performance, while coaches need to understand how their efforts contribute to their incentives.

    Designing the incentive calculator reinforced the importance of looking beyond the requested requirements and identifying opportunities to make a product more useful for its users.

    The project brought together performance configuration, evaluation, and individual visibility into one connected experience across FitWay CRM and the COREO Coach App.

    FitWay | COREO · Case study

    Designing a connected fitness experience that goes beyond the gym floor.

    COREO Client App: a premium fitness application that helps members manage their gym experience, book services, track fitness progress and stay connected with their fitness community.

    • What I did Designed the COREO Client App from the ground up: separate member and non-member journeys, a numerical FitScore out of 1,000, six goal-based PT session types with coach-availability-based booking, an adaptive feedback system, Body Composition and Transformation tracking, Community, plus COREO’s visual identity.
    • Outcome One connected digital experience linking members, coaches, the FitWay CRM, the iPad assessment and the body composition machine.

    Project Overview

    COREO is a premium fitness and wellness brand built on FitWay’s existing fitness management ecosystem. It needed its own client-facing app: not just a place to check membership details, but a digital companion for the whole fitness journey. I designed it from the ground up.

    COREO is a premium fitness and wellness brand built on FitWay’s existing fitness management ecosystem. FitWay already handled gym operations and member management, but COREO needed its own client-facing app that reflected its brand: not just a place to check membership details, but a digital companion for the whole fitness journey, from booking sessions to tracking progress and engaging with the community.

    I designed the COREO Client App from the ground up, using the FitWay ecosystem as a reference while creating a distinct experience for the COREO brand.

    While the existing FitWay ecosystem already supported gym operations and member management, COREO needed a new client-facing digital experience that reflected its own brand identity and product vision.

    The goal was to create more than an application for checking membership details. COREO wanted a digital companion that members could use throughout their fitness journey: from booking training sessions and accessing gym services to tracking physical progress and engaging with the community.

    I was responsible for designing the COREO Client App from the ground up, using the existing FitWay ecosystem as a reference while creating a distinct experience for the COREO brand.

    The Challenge

    Fitness management existed, but the member experience needed a new direction.

    Through stakeholder discussions, conversations with coaches and members, and my own experience as a gym member, I found five problems: a fragmented member experience, limited progress visibility, low engagement, feedback that was just a star rating and an empty box, and the need for a scalable ecosystem.

    The existing FitWay Client App offered several functionalities, but many members found it difficult to discover and use them effectively. At the same time, COREO wanted to establish a premium digital experience that could support its fitness ecosystem and future business expansion.

    Through stakeholder discussions, conversations with coaches and members, and my own experience as a gym member, I identified five key challenges.

    01 · Fragmented member experience

    Members needed to interact with the front desk and coaches for different activities, bookings and information. The digital experience needed to bring these interactions together.

    02 · Limited progress visibility

    Members needed a more structured way to understand their fitness performance and see how their efforts translated into measurable improvements.

    03 · Limited member engagement

    The application needed to become a useful part of members’ everyday gym routines rather than something they opened only when necessary.

    04 · Missing structured feedback

    The existing feedback experience offered only a five-star rating and an empty text box. It didn’t guide members to share specific feedback about their experiences.

    05 · A scalable digital ecosystem

    COREO wanted a distinct product experience that could support future expansion while connecting members, coaches and gym operations.

    The opportunity
    Transform multiple gym interactions into one connected digital fitness experience.

    Understanding Users and Business

    I approached the project from both sides: the business running the gym, which wanted regular app engagement, measurable progress and better service quality, and the members using it, who wanted to book, track progress and find information without asking the front desk.

    My discovery approach

    • Discussions with gym owners, stakeholders and coaches.
    • Conversations with some gym members and observing how they used gym services.
    • Reviewing the existing FitWay Client App.
    • Informal usability checks to see whether members could discover and use features on their own.
    I also joined the gym as a member to experience the product from the user’s perspective. This helped me understand everyday motivations, frustrations and small achievements that influence how people stay consistent with fitness.

    Defining the Product Experience

    Rather than redesigning existing screens, I built the app around the member’s fitness journey, with four main areas: Home, Book, Progress and Community. Members and non-members get different experiences.

    Rather than simply redesigning existing screens, I focused on creating a connected experience around the member’s fitness journey. I structured the application around four primary navigation areas.

    Home · Book · Progress · Community

    Home

    A personalised dashboard for accessing important gym information, fitness updates and frequently used services.

    Book

    A central place to reserve personal training, group sessions, fitness classes and wellness facilities.

    Progress

    A dedicated space for tracking FitScore assessments, weight and body composition measurements, and physical transformation.

    Community

    A space for members to share content, interact with posts and explore gym activities.

    Profile and account-related settings are accessible through the profile avatar rather than a separate bottom navigation item.

    Two different user experiences

    The app needed to serve both members and people who had not yet purchased a membership.

    • Member experience: focused on bookings, progress tracking, membership management, feedback and community engagement.
    • Non-member experience: focused on discovering COREO, understanding its services, exploring memberships and finding a suitable way to join.

    Home — A Personalised Fitness Dashboard

    Home brings frequently used information together: membership and booking shortcuts, FitScore, Body Composition, today’s workout, gym visits, nutrition, community and the PT session OTP, so members don’t dig through menus.

    The Home screen acts as the starting point for the member’s daily gym experience. I brought together frequently accessed information and important activities so members could understand their current fitness journey without navigating through multiple sections.

    Key elements

    • Membership and booking shortcuts.
    • COREO FitScore.
    • Body Composition Analysis.
    • Today’s workout.
    • Gym visit information.
    • Nutrition.
    • Community content.
    • Personal Training session details and verification OTP.

    The aim was to put frequently used features directly on Home, keep FitScore, workouts and body measurements in view to support engagement, and reduce menu-hunting. Non-members get a separate Home that introduces COREO’s proposition, facilities, services and memberships instead.

    Design Decisions — Improving Real Member Experiences

    Going beyond arranging screens to solve specific product problems.

    Three decisions shaped the product: a numerical FitScore out of 1,000 instead of labels like Poor, Good and Best; six goal-based Personal Training session types booked into each coach’s real slots, instead of one generic session in one-hour blocks; and an adaptive, emoji-based feedback flow with activity-specific follow-up questions instead of a star rating and an empty box.

    These were three important design decisions where I went beyond arranging screens and focused on solving specific product problems.

    Decision 01 — Making fitness progress measurable

    From “Good” to 654: making progress visible.

    The challenge: the previous fitness assessment described each exercise with a label: Poor, Good or Best. A label gave a rough idea, but a member who improved could still see “Good” month after month, so real progress stayed invisible.

    My approach: I replaced the labels with a numerical FitScore. Each of the 10 exercises is scored out of 100, and the total, out of 1,000, is the member’s FitScore. The same score is broken down into four fitness indicators, so members can see where it comes from.

    10 exercises × 100 → FitScore out of 1,000 → 4 indicators

    Before — a label

    Every exercise rated Poor, Good or Best.

    No overall picture, and no way to tell whether you improved since last month.

    After — one score

    654 /1000

    Strength 164 · Endurance 163 · Mobility 164 · Balance 163

    What members see: the Progress tab opens on FitScore AI Test, alongside Weight and Transformation.

    • A score ring with the test date and the four indicators.
    • A short encouraging message under the score, so a result reads as a step in the journey rather than a verdict.
    • “Your FitScore Journey”, a month-by-month graph of every assessment.
    • A clear “Take FitScore AI Test” action.
    • FitScore history by year, where each assessment expands to show all 10 exercise scores.

    Decision 02 — Making Personal Training personal and precise

    From one session for everyone to the right session, coach and time.

    The challenge: Personal Training was a single, one-size-fits-all offering, whatever the member’s goal. Booking it meant choosing from generic one-hour slots, such as 8–9 AM, 9–10 AM and 10–11 AM, that didn’t reflect any individual coach’s actual availability. Members ended up coordinating through coaches, the front desk and the app at the same time.

    My approach: I worked on this in two parts. First, I split Personal Training into six session types, each built around a goal. Second, I designed a booking journey built around each coach’s real open time, synchronised across FitWay CRM, Coach App and Client App, so only slots that can actually be booked appear.

    Weight Loss & Transformation45 minLosing weight, restarting fitness
    Muscle Building & Strength45 minMuscle size and strength
    Functional Fitness & Endurance45 minMovement control and stamina
    Special Population & Rehab45 minSeniors, post-injury, joint issues
    Dance Fitness45 minA fun, energetic alternative
    Nutritionist30 minWeight and nutrition guidance

    Each session type has its own page with the same structure: goal, what the session includes, duration, who it’s best for and expected outcomes, then a “Book Now” action. Members can compare options on equal terms and pick what fits them, instead of booking a generic session and explaining their goal afterwards.

    Session type → Date → Coach → Open slot

    Before — one size for all

    One Personal Training type for every member, booked into the same one-hour blocks whether or not a coach was free.

    Confirming a session often meant messaging the coach or asking the front desk.

    After — the right fit

    Choose one of six goal-based session types, then a date, a coach filtered by specialty, and only that coach’s open slots.

    Book and get instant confirmation, with no approval step.

    What members see:

    • A week strip to pick the date, with month navigation.
    • Coaches shown with photo and specialty, filterable by HIIT, Strength, Cardio or Yoga.
    • Slots grouped into Morning, Afternoon and Evening, with unavailable times greyed out instead of hidden, so members can see how the day looks.
    • A clear note that each slot is 45 minutes, and one “Book Now” action.

    Decision 03 — Making member feedback more meaningful

    From a star and an empty box to feedback coaches can act on.

    The challenge: the existing feedback experience offered only a five-star rating and an empty text box. Members could rate a session or write a comment, but nothing guided them towards specific, actionable feedback, and an empty box asks a lot of someone who has just finished a workout.

    My approach: I designed an adaptive feedback experience: five emoji-based rating levels, where the rating a member picks decides the tone and the follow-up questions they see, and the questions themselves are written for each activity.

    Rate → Tap what fits → Add a note → Share

    Before — rate and write

    Five stars and one empty text box, the same for every activity.

    Ratings said how a session felt, but rarely why.

    After — rate and tap

    One tap on an emoji, then quick chips matched to the rating and the activity.

    Writing is optional, and members can come back later.

    One tap, then the right question
    😖WorstWhat went wrong?
    😔BadWhat could be better?
    😐NeutralWhat worked well, and what could be better?
    😁GoodWhat stood out for you?
    🤩AwesomeWhat made it exceptional?

    From the design · neutral asks both sides, because a “fine” session usually has something to keep and something to fix

    What members see:

    • A bottom sheet after the session: “How do you feel after your session today?” with five emojis.
    • Choosing one expands the sheet with a title in the matching tone, from an apology for a poor session to a celebration for a great one.
    • Tappable chips for the reasons, so most feedback takes a few seconds.
    • “My reason is not available here” and an optional suggestions box for anything the chips miss.
    • “Share Your Experience” to submit, or “Remind me later” for members who are short on time.

    Asking new members what they actually want

    I reused the same chip pattern for a one-time preference prompt. In a member’s first month, once they’ve attended each of Yoga, Dance Fitness and Group Personal Training at least once, the app asks which group classes they prefer.

    Try all three → Pick favourites → Relevant alerts

    COREO then sends class notifications only for what they picked, so members hear about the sessions they care about and aren’t disturbed by updates for the rest. Asking after they’ve tried all three, not on day one, means the choice is based on experience rather than a guess.

    A star tells you how a session felt. The right question tells you why.

    FitScore AI — Understanding Fitness Performance

    COREO already had AI posture detection. I designed the experience around it: a self-paced iPad assessment, repeated monthly, with history, exercise-wise breakdowns and reminder notifications.

    COREO already had an AI-based posture-detection capability used during fitness assessments. My contribution was designing the experience around it, so the assessment felt understandable and motivating rather than purely technical.

    Members complete the assessment through an iPad-based camera experience. The system evaluates posture automatically, and incorrect posture affects the exercise score. There is no time limit, so members go at their own pace through exercises such as Chair Pose Side, Modified Push-up, Plank, Pull-up, Sit & Reach and Squats.

    The assessment repeats monthly. Members see their journey in the progress views from Decision 01, can open any previous assessment to review all 10 exercise scores, and receive a push notification reminding them when the next one is due.

    Body Composition & Transformation

    Connecting physical assessments with digital progress.

    Body Composition machine data syncs automatically to the app, Coach App and CRM, and members get a notification. Members can view or compare records, and see coach-uploaded transformation photos by date.

    Fitness progress isn’t only about exercise performance. The Body Composition Analysis machine is the source of measurement data, and results sync automatically, so nobody types numbers in by hand.

    Machine assessment → Automatic data sync → Client App update → Member notification

    For members

    Weight, body fat percentage, BMI and other measurements, with history. Members can view a single record or compare two dates.

    For coaches

    Coaches see the same data in the COREO Coach App and CRM, for all COREO Balewadi members, but can’t edit machine-generated measurements.

    Transformation

    Coaches upload transformation photos through the Coach App. Members pick dates to compare photos and measurements side by side.

    Booking — Bringing Gym Services into One Experience

    Four service types, each with its own rules, in one consistent booking flow: Personal Training, Group Personal Training, Fitness Classes and the Ceragem wellness bed.

    Members needed to book different services without manual coordination every time. Each service has different eligibility, scheduling and payment rules, so the challenge was making those rules understandable within one consistent booking experience.

    Personal Training

    Six goal-based session types, active packages and the upcoming session, then coach-specific slots (see Decision 02). Members without an eligible package are told one is required and given a route to buy it.

    Group Personal Training

    Group workouts with heart-rate tracking through COREOBeat Zones and a Group HR Training Band assigned by the coach. Members book and cancel in the app.

    Fitness Classes

    Yoga, Dance Fitness and Pilates, included in the membership, with flexible schedules set by the lead coach.

    Ceragem Bed

    A paid wellness booking with its own flow: pick a slot, review and pay. Yearly members get one free session a month.

    Community & the Non-member Journey

    Members share photos and videos with their gym community. Non-members get a discovery-focused Home with COREO’s services, public videos managed from the CRM, and two ways to join: buy in the app or visit the sales team.

    Community — a shared gym experience

    Members can create posts using photos or videos, like and comment on posts, and explore content shared by members at the same gym location. The experience is location-based, so members at COREO Balewadi see content from their gym community. It is a content-sharing experience rather than a full social network: friend requests and connections are not supported.

    For non-members, the Home page includes selected public videos uploaded by COREO, showcasing gym facilities, workout experiences, classes and services. The COREO team manages which videos are visible through the CRM, connecting its content workflow with the app’s public discovery experience.

    From discovery to membership

    Instead of the member dashboard, non-members get a discovery-oriented Home introducing COREO’s AI-powered gym, performance zones, memberships, services, coaches and location.

    I considered two ways of purchasing a membership:

    1. Direct purchase through the application.
    2. Visiting the gym and discussing membership options with the sales team.

    The second option mattered because some customers prefer personal assistance before buying. This let the digital experience complement the existing sales process instead of forcing everyone into a single purchase journey. When a non-member buys a membership with their existing account, it becomes a member account and keeps their login and profile.

    Connecting the Digital Ecosystem

    The Client App works alongside the COREO Coach App, FitWay CRM, the iPad assessment and the Body Composition machine. I designed for how data moves between them, not just for individual screens.

    One of the most interesting aspects of this project was that the Client App did not operate independently. It was part of a larger fitness ecosystem involving multiple touchpoints.

    COREO Client App

    The member-facing experience for bookings, fitness progress, membership and community.

    COREO Coach App

    Supports coach workflows, member information and transformation photo uploads.

    FitWay CRM

    Supports gym operations, booking availability, member management and content management.

    iPad Assessment

    Used for the camera-based FitScore AI assessment.

    Body Composition Machine

    Provides automated physical measurement data.

    Coach availability in the CRM drives booking slots, iPad assessments and machine measurements flow into the app, and CRM-managed videos feed the non-member Home. Designing it meant thinking about how information moves, not just individual screens.

    Visual Design and Brand Experience

    COREO needed to feel different from FitWay. I created the COREO logo and brand identity, the typography and colour direction, light and dark themes, and the reusable components and icons used across the app. I also carried the brand into print and marketing material, though this case study focuses on how the visual system supports the product experience.

I kept checking whether members could use features without help, and raised improvements with stakeholders, developers and testers. For example, I identified that FitScore reminders and body composition notifications should open the relevant screen directly, and communicated that requirement to the team.

    Key Takeaways

    • Joining the gym myself showed me members are motivated by small improvements, convenience and visible progress.
    • A booking screen depends on coach availability, package eligibility and connected systems, so good design means understanding those relationships.
    • Showing a number isn’t enough: members need context, history and a way to understand what it means for their journey.

    Designing the COREO Client App helped me understand that a fitness application is much more than a collection of booking and tracking screens. The real challenge was connecting different parts of the gym experience into one understandable digital journey.

    COREO moved me from designing screens to designing a system: real workflows, connected platforms and everyday behaviour. Small decisions, from a fitness score to a booking confirmation or a feedback question, add up to a more connected fitness experience.
